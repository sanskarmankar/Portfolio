AI/ML intern to quant 

I build ML systems end to end. I've fine-tuned an 8B medical Q&A model, served it on CUDA, and built a FAISS-based RAG pipeline that keeps answers grounded in source PDFs. I've also shipped a 97% helmet detector (YOLOv8), an emotion classifier, and a resume screener behind FastAPI services.

By day I was a full-time intern. By evening I was building an intraday EMA/SMA trend-filter strategy for the Indian market. Backtests showed a 70% win rate, with a 30-point stop and 20-point target, and drawdowns capped near 250 points. With ₹20k, I traded it through options buying, and that's where I learned my most valuable lesson: I had backtested on the index, not on options, so theta quietly ate most of my edge. I survived 8 months, then an overnight hold on election day wiped the account. The strategy wasn't the problem. My instrument choice and risk management were. Now I'd use futures or directional option selling, backtest on the actual instrument, and never carry overnight risk unhedged. I lost only what I could afford to lose, and it taught me more about risk than any course could.

PHASE 02 2024 — 2025

The Transition: Mathematical & statistical  Foundations

Untrade Quantitative researcher • Jaipur , India

Backtesting , Genetic algorithm I trading , Alpha creation 

My first serious step into quantitative research started with Bitcoin.

I joined Untrade through its Create & Earn program after independently developing a BTC alpha strategy built around Hawkes process modeling. The strategy focused on detecting volatility expansion rather than trying to predict every market move. It qualified with a 68% win rate, 1:3 risk-to-reward ratio, and Sharpe ratio above 6, giving me the opportunity to work as a Quantitative Researcher.

What started as one strategy quickly became a much broader research problem: how do you know whether a backtest is actually telling you something real?

I worked on Ethereum alpha models, event-driven statistical signals, and systematic parameter optimization. I built a 5-island genetic algorithm with adaptive mutation and parallel evaluation using ThreadPoolExecutor, allowing multiple parameter populations to evolve independently while reducing research time.

More importantly, I became obsessive about the part of quantitative research that is easy to overlook: making sure the edge isn't an artifact of the backtest.

I developed protocols for detecting look-ahead/front-running bias, enforced strict in-sample/out-of-sample validation, and used Monte Carlo simulations to estimate risk and understand how sensitive strategies were to different return paths. I also built automated data-extraction and research pipelines so that strategy development could move from raw data → signal → backtest → validation → risk analysis with minimal manual intervention.

This was where I stopped thinking of trading as simply finding a strategy that makes money and started thinking like a researcher: form a hypothesis, test it without contaminating the data, stress it, try to break it, and only then decide whether the result is worth trusting.
